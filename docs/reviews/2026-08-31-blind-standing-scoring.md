**Rule 3 stops scoring; the following is my own judgment, not the rule's output, and it is conditional on S-a through S-e being resolved. The author's declaration of materiality overrides it.**

**I would select the terminal lineage record.**

Four reasons, in descending weight:

1. **The witness's benefit does not reach the party this document is about.** The verifier the criteria govern holds one bundle, no network (G2), possibly eighty years out. For that verifier the witness entry is a TLR with extra signatures — its own G4 sentence concedes it "cannot establish log uniqueness … unless the trust configuration already asserts a single consistent log **and the verifier can reach it**," and reaching it is precisely what G2 excludes from the standing report. The equivocation closure accrues to a real-time auditor. Paying a permanent custody cost for a benefit that lands outside the standing report's threat model is a bad trade.

3. **Selecting the TLR changes nothing about the project's existing risk posture; selecting the witness changes its survivability posture.** The TLR's residual (F3) is exactly the residue A2.4 already leaves open by design and A3 §A3.7.1 confines without closing. The author's paragraph 2 also indicates the project's declared answer to entitled-key compromise lives elsewhere (bounded key lifetime, tombstone, forward-link generation chain) — cited as author-stated intent, with his own uncertainty flagged, not as registered text. Buying partial closure a second time inside the standing mechanism is the more disruptive move.

And I would enter the selection only with the F1 well-formedness check, an F2 ruling, and a written temporal boundary sentence attached as dated amendments.

## 4. The strongest good-faith case AGAINST each survivor

### Against the terminal lineage record

**It solves the easy half of the problem and presents the result as clean.** The entire reason A3.7.1 exists is that cryptographic validity confers no standing; the reason standing is *hard* is that the entitled key can equivocate. The TLR is defeated by exactly one adversary — the one whose key the whole scheme trusts — and the record says so in its own words: F3, "a TLR closes 'which of two valid artifacts did the issuer designate' against everyone **except the issuer key itself**." Q5 shows both contradictory bundles returning `ESTABLISHED` with the identical reason code, and `run2.out` states flatly that a lone verifier "has no way to see the other TLR." C3 asks whether a relying party sees `ABSENT`/`UNVERIFIABLE` with a reason or a false `ESTABLISHED`; under the construction's own named boundary condition, the TLR delivers a false `ESTABLISHED`, silently, to both parties, and cannot be made to do otherwise. A proponent must explain why C1 simplicity should outrank a registered comparative criterion scoring at its failing pole on the case the mechanism exists to address — and must do so without leaning on "A2 left it open anyway," because A3 §A3.7.1 confining a residue is not the same as a *newly selected mechanism* declining to touch it.

**G4's temporal clause was added for a shape exactly like this one, and the sketch does not answer it.** The TLR is created after the anchor confirms, is bound to nothing in time (its body is `binding`/`lineage`/`terminal`; the one anchor-adjacent field, the per-attempt `anchor_ref`, was found "not load-bearing … the verifier never read it"), and is carried by whoever holds it. In the author's own work case the adversary is not a live attacker but a leaked key and a record fabricated decades later — and the TLR verifier cannot distinguish a TLR signed at terminal disposition from one signed in 2106 by a key nobody survives to revoke. The artifact it vouches for *is* temporally bounded, by A2.1's declared-time-within-δ predicate, checked in the probe (`confirmed_at 55 ≤ declared 0 + δ 60`). The standing evidence that determines which artifact counts has weaker temporal grounding than the artifacts it adjudicates between. G4 asked for that boundary to be named; the sketch names only the equivocation one. The proponent must either anchor the TLR — a second anchoring event that spends the C1 advantage that is the main case for it — or write down plainly that standing evidence is unbounded in time, and then defend selecting it anyway.

**The probe's evidence does not discriminate the TLR from its rival, and rule 5 plus §7 forbid pretending otherwise.** What Q3 established is that verifier-computed identity is load-bearing: the ordinal variant "is not a weaker binding; it is no binding, and it accepts every transplant." That is now a requirement G1 imposes on *every* candidate, and the witness sketch inherits it under §1's forward reference. Q2 and Q4 establish that a signed lineage of (identity, disposition) pairs discriminates S1/S2/S3 — a property the witness entry has by construction, being "a TLR published." So every green line in the probe is evidence about TLR-*shaped* constructions in general. §7 explicitly excludes "convenience arising merely because one candidate was prototyped," and rule 5 bars using the witness's lack of a probe against it. Strip out what is really shared, and the TLR's remaining case is C1/C2 — a complexity-and-custody argument — which is precisely the branch rule 3 says does *not* dispose of a fork where the survivors differ materially on security.

**Two mandatory properties are unsatisfied as it stands, and both defects were found in the TLR's own evidence.** R3 enumerates five failure categories and demands "no inference, fallback, or normalization"; the sketch's verifier list has three checks, and F1 shows internally inconsistent evidence — a TLR naming attempt 1 shipped while its own lineage entry for attempt 1 reads `ABANDONED_ISSUER_TIMEOUT` — returning `ESTABLISHED`. That is not a missing outcome; it is a *wrong* one, in the direction C3 calls the bad pole. R2 requires the relationship to the portable refusal record to be explicit and non-contradictable; Q7 found "overlap, not subset" in both directions and F2 found a fourth standing condition S1–S3 do not cover. Under rule 1 an unsatisfied §3 property is a *rejection*, not a demerit. Selecting the TLR now means selecting a construction that fails two registered mandatory properties as sketched, on the promise that the amendments are cheap — and "cheap to amend" is not a criterion in this document.

### Against the transparency witness

**It buys its advantage with the one currency the project has declared it will not spend.** The survivability floor (§0, A3 §A3.4) limits guarantees to what holds "while an observer holding the relevant state survives," and makes continued availability after handoff conditional on custody. The author's registered C4 paragraph 3 states the constraint in the strongest terms available to him: the design "insists this *does not include any dependency on any Tessera service surviving*," and the whole documented-format strategy exists so a third party can act on the bundle eighty years out. C2's sharp question is whether a construction creates a new custody subject; the witness does — a log that must be operated, retained, and served — and C2 answers what happens next: "that subject then has no assigned owner (band-1 docket item 17): retention and retrieval responsibility for anything a verifier must fetch from outside the envelope is currently unassigned." Selecting it therefore does not merely add a component; it manufactures an unowned obligation and forces open a docket item, inside a decision whose §5 has no mechanism for assigning owners.

**The closure it buys is not delivered to the party the whole document is about.** Its own G4 sentence is the strongest argument against it: a verifier holding one bundle "cannot establish log uniqueness — that no second head, or second entry under the same head, carries a contradictory disposition — unless the trust configuration already asserts a single consistent log and the verifier can reach it." Reaching it is a live query, exactly what G2 excludes from the standing report. So for the offline adjudicator the witness is a TLR carrying two extra signatures and a Merkle path, with identical equivocation exposure; the benefit accrues to a hypothetical auditor monitoring the log while Tessera is alive — the period during which the equivocation problem is *least* acute, because a living authority can still revoke. A proponent must explain why the decision should pay a permanent custody cost for a benefit that peaks precisely when it is least needed and vanishes exactly when G4's temporal clause says the risk is worst.

**Its trust-configuration demand may not be admissible without first amending a pinned statement.** §0 defines the trust configuration by enumeration — channel public keys, `k`, `δ`, governing specification and policy — as "an input, not a fetch," restating A3 §A3.8, which §6 pins at `8ae4720`. A log public key or Merkle root is none of the enumerated items. If admitting one requires amending A3 §A3.8, §6 makes that a rule-4 event: "the criteria are re-registered before scoring resumes, not silently reread." That is an ordering defect no amount of comparative scoring repairs — the witness cannot be selected under these criteria until a registered statement changes, whereas the TLR needs nothing added to the verifier's pre-bundle inputs at all (Q6: the pre-bundle set is `dns`, `repo`, `k`, `δ`, and nothing else).

**Its gate passes are the youngest, least exercised, and thinnest in the field — and rule 5 cuts both ways.** Its G0 pass rests on a correction made the same day as registration: an earlier sketch "recorded only the terminal disposition in the entry while the G0 story claimed lineage was bound," caught by the third blind reviewer — whom this document itself records as the weakest independence test, "a same-model check … weaker as an independence test than the other two (A1.7's correlated-blind-spot concern)." Rule 5 rightly forbids failing the witness for lacking a probe; it equally forbids crediting it with the TLR's. Its transplant companion (G1) and collapsing control (G3) have never been run, and its *added* failure surface — malformed entry, invalid inclusion proof, unknown or rotated head, internally inconsistent entry — carries no reason codes in the sketch at all, a strictly larger R3 gap than the TLR's single missing well-formedness check. Finally, R1 is mandatory and gates Band 0 exit under panel criterion 4: the witness needs the TLR's whole model *plus* an inclusion-proof encoding, and R1 pins the theory to the first-link declarations and caps the model at "a size the author can read," roughly a hundred lines. A construction whose formal obligation is the larger one, whose failure taxonomy is the less specified one, and whose distinguishing benefit lands outside the offline verifier's reach is asking to be selected on the strength of a property it does not deliver to the reader the criteria were written for.
