# Amendment 12 review — findings (2026-11-17)

## A12-01 — High: the deadline overrides the latch and shipping obligation without registering that change

`docs/phase-0-prereg-amendment-12.md:24-34` ends **every** non-COMPLETE request at 14 days, including a timely-latched attempt stuck in final assembly. A2.1 promises that eligibility latched within the envelope cannot be expired by later packaging or scheduling delay, makes eligible/finalizing and refused mutually exclusive, and obliges shipment of every timely-eligible attempt (`docs/phase-0-prereg-amendment-2.md:130-145,150-160`); A2.3 repeats the exclusion (`:260-264`). These sentences are not quoted as changed. For example, a qualifying presentation latches on day 2, then the object store is unavailable through day 14: A12 refuses it despite its latch and its express statement that the latch rule does not change. The lifecycle design expressly preserves and retries such an attempt (`docs/decisions/2026-10-29-issuance-lifecycle.md:103-105,150-155`). The author must decide whether the deadline defeats that protection and shipping obligation, with the affected registered sentences quoted and the loss stated. The author's question quoted at A12:20-21 identifies a concern; it does not settle this specific tradeoff.

## A12-02 — High: elapsed request time does not establish excess lag or absent standing

`docs/phase-0-prereg-amendment-12.md:28-30` asserts the A2.1 excess-lag trigger even when later evidence proves timeliness. A request deadline measures time since acceptance, not B − C; an attempt can be timely observed and latched, or its timely observation can fail to persist. The lifecycle record identifies both the observation-versus-burial ambiguity and these protected cases (`docs/decisions/2026-10-29-issuance-lifecycle.md:108-134`). A2.1's permission is conditional, not a rule that turns a timely attempt late as time passes. A2.4 confines standing of an anchor **not shipped inside a receipt**, while preserving evidentiary admissibility (`docs/phase-0-prereg-amendment-2.md:307-315`); V1 independently reports standing absent (`docs/V1-CONTRACT.md:31-33,49`). If the author wants a new terminal-disposition rule for timely artifacts, specify it as a change rather than an inferred fact, and preserve the distinction between cryptographic validity, admissibility and standing. The assertion that this is “never a safety loss” (`A12:31-32`) also presumes effective exclusion of a late shipment; see A12-03.

## A12-03 — High: a deadline declaration does not establish a durable terminal transition or prevent a late shipment

`docs/phase-0-prereg-amendment-12.md:24-27,35-37` supplies no failure model, atomic boundary or treatment of in-flight completion and external delivery. If the lifecycle writer or durable store is unavailable across L_term, no refusal can be durably recorded at the deadline; a wall clock alone does not make the transition. Conversely, a worker can start completion before L_term, stall, and publish or return the receipt after another worker refuses unless the final disposition and delivery are fenced. A2.3 requires durable refusal and atomic final expiry/refusal (`docs/phase-0-prereg-amendment-2.md:265-268,278-289`); the design relies on a single writer, guarded attempt IDs and fencing (`docs/decisions/2026-10-29-issuance-lifecycle.md:47-52,72-74,98-101,157-164`). Specify the deadline tie, durable decision, stale-write and delivery rules and the availability/fairness assumptions under which a real-time bound is possible. A future TLA+ check is an implementation duty, not an existing proof; its model must include these failure and delivery paths. If permanent storage or process failure is in scope, a strict durable deadline cannot be promised without an independent available mechanism.

## A12-04 — Medium: the 14-day calculation is not a scheduling guarantee, and closure is sooner

`docs/phase-0-prereg-amendment-12.md:24-25` correctly computes 3 × 96 h = 12 days, but that is a nominal sum from each attempt's declared time, not a bound from acceptance; waiting to start, between attempts, or after a latch is unbounded (`docs/phase-0-prereg-amendment-2.md:270-274`; lifecycle decision `:34-35`). The extra two days do not establish those delays fit. At the last L1 acceptance, nine days before not-after, signing closes five days later, so a third attempt needing a signature after that stop is refused `SIGNING_CLOSED` rather than enjoying three full attempts (`docs/V1-SERVICE-RESOLUTIONS.md:42`; lifecycle decision `:157-164`). L_term then falls five days after not-after. Existing signed and stamped work can still be processed subject to the actual T check; no new attempt may be signed after the stop. Make the 14 days an author-selected cap and explain this interaction, not a derived operating guarantee. The lifecycle's sampled-failure `EVIDENCE_UNAVAILABLE` rule also ends an unlatched attempt and may allocate another, whereas A12 ends the whole request at a fixed deadline; enabling the existing gated path without defining which rule governs would implement a different policy (`docs/decisions/2026-10-29-issuance-lifecycle.md:80-105,157-164,189-194`).

## A12-05 — High: replacing DNS after signing stops can strand an old in-flight receipt

`docs/phase-0-prereg-amendment-12.md:52-54` permits replacement immediately after the old signing stop and says replacement does not affect receipts. A completed receipt with a usable historical snapshot can indeed verify independently of current DNS. But a signed and stamped old attempt may still be finalizing (`docs/V1-SERVICE-RESOLUTIONS.md:42`); if its old snapshot is missing or fails its time checks, recapture after replacement yields the successor value, which cannot match its old git assertion and keys. The verifier requires matching authority values (`docs/V1-PROFILE.md:792`) and a snapshot anchored within the receipt's declared-time interval, with timely confirmation (`docs/V1-PROFILE.md:928-947`). A snapshot cannot necessarily be refreshed indefinitely even before replacement. Thus an old timely-latched attempt can lose its shipping opportunity; A12-01's deadline can eventually refuse it. Define a cutover that accounts for outstanding attempts and usable preserved evidence, or explicitly ask the author to accept and register the lost opportunity. One TXT record and the same owner/path are compatible with the frozen checks (`docs/V1-PROFILE.md:638-643`), but do not solve this in-flight case.

## A12-06 — High: the proposed gap rejection and model property overstate the verifier

`docs/phase-0-prereg-amendment-12.md:57-60` requires a negative vector for any receipt whose T is in the gap and a model claiming no receipt under a window's keys outside that window. A10.2 explicitly says the verifier does not compare T with not-before, does not bound declared time above by not-after, and does not establish when signing occurred (`docs/phase-0-prereg-amendment-10.md:358-373`; frozen profile `docs/V1-PROFILE.md:723-729`). For example, successor not-before is 100, old not-after is 99, declared = 100, T = 99.5 and confirmation satisfies A2's bounds: successor C4 accepts T in the gap, assuming other checks and chain-order evidence pass. The gap vector therefore cannot demand rejection solely because T is in the gap; old-window T in the gap is rejected as above old not-after. Similarly the frozen verifier does not compare windows across receipts, so distinct inclusive windows do not prove global non-overlap or detect malicious republishing (`docs/phase-0-prereg-amendment-9.md:411-416`). Define which issuer event and clock the intended model constrains, and register vectors that test actual predicates; retain the separate chain-order condition `h_git + 5 < h_receipt` (`docs/V1-PROFILE.md:798-808`). Confirmation before first issuance alone should not replace that check.

## A12-07 — Medium: the stated continuity and scope require author choices

`docs/phase-0-prereg-amendment-12.md:62-64` promises a gap no longer than pre-commitment time. L1 stops old acceptance at least 216 h before the 2027-02-01 endpoint, i.e. no later than 2027-01-23 00:00 UTC; successor not-before must be strictly after February 1 (`service/issuer/config/window.json:17`; A12:50). Even if pre-commitment was completed weeks early, this creates at least nine days without accepting new requests under those window rules, plus the strict endpoint gap; preparation does not eliminate it. Moreover confirmation and dependency recovery have no stated finite bound. Distinguish acceptance, signing and completion of old work, and state the actual downtime rather than a pre-commitment-time bound. The author must choose whether the availability gap, the 14-day cutoff including protected work, and any irreversible impact on already accepted requests are acceptable. A9's future dated registration and model are prerequisites, not evidence that the vectors or model have already passed (`docs/phase-0-prereg-amendment-9.md:415-418`).

## A12-08 — Low: the change inventory omits and mislocates registered text

The A2 quotations at `docs/phase-0-prereg-amendment-12.md:11-15` are accurately located, but the inventory omits the conflicting latch, shipment and expiry sentences identified in A12-01 and the anchor qualification in A12-02. The A9 quotation at `A12:41-43` includes “Without that registration…” from `docs/phase-0-prereg-amendment-9.md:417-418`, outside its stated `:409-417` locator. The quoted first-window prohibition is accurate, and the proposed same owner/path, fresh keys, strict endpoint ordering and future dated registration correspond to A9:411-418; the model and vectors are still future work. Update the locator and enumerate every altered registered sentence, including A10.2 if the intended gap claim changes its narrow verifier semantics. Also reconcile the frozen profile's descriptive “one window” (`docs/V1-PROFILE.md:725-726`) without implying its byte/check rules have already been changed.

## Owner prechecks

1. **Capture row — FAIL (qualification missing).** The broad successor statements and model at `docs/phase-0-prereg-amendment-12.md:45-60` do not state their authority assumption. If DNS and git are both captured, an attacker can publish its own keys and window and mint fresh receipts that pass; neither rotation nor a one-record rule detects capture (`docs/V1-CONTRACT.md:34-37,65-73`; `docs/V1-PROFILE.md:620-627`). Honest historical receipts retain the separately conditioned G10 protection. The text does not explicitly claim capture detection, but the unqualified “no receipt ... outside” claim omits the relevant attacker.
2. **Trigger clause — FAIL.** A12:28-30 treats expiry of the request deadline as proof that observation lag exceeded S, although the actual A2.1 trigger need not occur (A12-02). A12:57-60 correctly makes the A9 rotation registration future and pre-issuance; it is not yet a completed registration.
3. **Requester — FAIL (scope missing).** A12:48-60 names new KMS and HSM keys and claims an issuance invariant without saying who may request signatures. An issuing-account attacker can enqueue frames for the independent policy signer and obtain both signatures within its clock policy (`docs/phase-0-prereg-amendment-9.md:382-388,401-404`; `docs/V1-CONTRACT.md:49-51,73`). Key replacement or custody alone does not enforce the proposed property against that requester. State the policy-signer boundary and adversary scope of the model.
4. **Binding text — FAIL.** A12:28-34 invokes A2.1's permission without its trigger and asserts the latch unchanged while contradicting A2.1/A2.3 as in A12-01 and A12-02. The L1 stops are owner operating controls, not registered guarantees (`docs/V1-SERVICE-RESOLUTIONS.md:42`; `docs/phase-0-prereg-amendment-9.md:422-423`); the 14-day bound and successor authorization remain unsigned proposals (`docs/phase-0-prereg-amendment-12.md:3-6`). The cited A2 termination obligation is registered, but it does not itself authorize cutting off protected eligibility.
